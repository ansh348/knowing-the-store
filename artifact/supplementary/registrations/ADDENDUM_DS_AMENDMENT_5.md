## §A5.1: Why: what the audit found and what this amendment tests

The audit (`reviews/DOWNSTREAM_AUDIT.md` step 4b; `reviews/downstream_audit/step4b_view_lines.json`; `AUX-DS-AUDIT-SOURCE`) found that for 35 of the 89 scorable obligations (21 supersession-derived, 14 retraction-derived) the answerer's view carries at least one **active** line whose text asserts the stale value: a record the store keeps active although the manifest's ground truth makes it a duplicate of superseded or retracted content. Every stale answer GPT-4.1 mini gave under the oracle fetch was on such a view (16 of 16), and the Amendment 3 instruction, which addresses tagged lines, could not touch them. Section 8 therefore says the fix is in the write path. This amendment performs that fix on the view alone: the duplicate lines are tagged with the state the manifest gives them, nothing else in any view changes, and the same two answerers re-answer every view that changed under the Amendment 4 header. What it measures is whether stale answers fall once the store's untagged duplicates carry their tag, with the fetch policies, the prompt, the detector and every other line held fixed.

## §A5.2: The change: tagging rule and renderer lock

**The duplicate lines.** For each of the 452 distinct (item, view) pairs of Amendment 4 whose item is a supersession- or retraction-derived obligation, a line is a duplicate iff it prints `lifecycle: active` and its text asserts the item's stale value by the item's own committed detector at TAU_GOLD (old-only coverage at least 0.50 with at least 2 shared tokens and new-only coverage below CROSS_GUARD 0.34; `amend4_header.py` `view_source`, `audit_ds.py` `step4b`), the rule the audit used to count the 35. The stale and gold fields come from the manifest join (`rec["stale_field"]`, `rec["gold_field"]`), so the tag is the manifest's: **`superseded`** for a supersession-derived obligation, **`retracted`** for a retraction-derived one. Expiry obligations are unscorable by that detector (their gold field is the expired fact) and no view of theirs changes; PRESENT views carry no obligation and do not change.

**The rewrite.** In each such line the `lifecycle: active` field becomes `lifecycle: superseded` or `lifecycle: retracted`; the rank, category, attribute, age and text are untouched; no other line of the view changes; no line is added or removed; the header and the prompt are Amendment 4's. RENDERER LOCK, asserted per view at build and again before any call: the new view has the same line count as the recorded view; every differing line differs only in the lifecycle field, from `active` to the item's state tag; the set of changed ranks equals the audit's `active_stale` ranks for that (item, policy) in `step4b_view_lines.json` for every policy the view serves (a mismatch is a STOP); and a view with no duplicate line is byte-identical to its recorded text and is not re-run.

**Population, computed from the recorded artifacts before the freeze (no call).** 74 distinct views change: 70 of the 422 in `run/cell_ds.json` (41 on supersession-derived, 29 on retraction-derived obligations; 124 lines tagged) and 4 of the 30 LOOKUP-LIT views of `run/rows_ds_amend2.jsonl`; they cover all 35 obligations (ORACLE 35 views, MODEL and MODEL-ALL 20, ALWAYS, LOOKUP and LOOKUP-ALL 17 each, by the policies each view serves). The runner recomputes these counts at build and a mismatch is a STOP. The other 378 views are unchanged and their Amendment 4 rows carry over unchanged.
